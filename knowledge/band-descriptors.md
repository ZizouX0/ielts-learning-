# Band descriptors — working paraphrase for grading

**What this is.** A restatement, in original words, of what bands 5, 6, 7 and 8+
actually look like for each Writing and Speaking criterion. Written from the
official public descriptor documents (Writing, updated May 2023; Speaking, public
version) — see `knowledge/sources.md` for links.

**What this is not.** The official text. Nothing here is quoted from the official
descriptors. For any official purpose, read the real documents.

**The governing rule.** A script or performance must **fully** fit a band's
positive features to earn that band. Partial fit means the band below. The
official documents also mark certain negative features that cap a rating however
strong the rest is — under-length, off-topic, memorised material, and
copying from the input prompt are the ones that bite in practice.

# WRITING TASK 1 (Academic)

## Task Achievement

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | Every requirement covered relevantly and sufficiently. Key features are chosen with skill, highlighted clearly, and illustrated. Only occasional small omissions. |
| **7** | All requirements of the task covered. Content is relevant and accurate, with perhaps a few small gaps or slips. Format is appropriate. |
| **6** | Focuses on what the task asks and uses an appropriate format. The key features chosen are covered and given adequate prominence, and **an overview is attempted**. Data is used to support the description. But some information may be irrelevant, inappropriate or inaccurate in the detail, and some points may need more extension — or there may be too much detail. |
| **5** | Generally addresses the task, but format may be wrong in places. Key features are not adequately covered; the recounting of detail is largely mechanical, and there may be **no data** supporting the description. |

**Where band 6 becomes band 7:** the overview stops being "attempted" and becomes
genuinely present and accurate; detail becomes selective rather than mechanical;
figures are quoted correctly.

**The single most expensive Task 1 mistake:** no overview. Writing that lists
numbers without a summary statement of the main trend cannot reach band 7 on
Task Achievement, no matter how good the English is.

## Coherence & Cohesion

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | Effortless to follow. Ideas logically sequenced, cohesion well managed and unobtrusive. Paragraphing is used well. |
| **7** | Logically organised with clear progression throughout; only a few minor lapses. Cohesive devices — including reference and substitution — used flexibly, though with some inaccuracy or some over/under-use. Paragraphing generally supports coherence. |
| **6** | Generally coherent with clear overall progression. Cohesive devices work to some good effect, but cohesion within or between sentences can be **faulty or mechanical** through misuse, overuse or omission. Reference and substitution may lack flexibility, producing repetition. |
| **5** | Organisation is visible but not wholly logical, and overall progression is weak — though an underlying sense of coherence survives. Ideas can be followed but sentences are not fluently linked. Cohesive devices are limited or overused. Paragraphing may be inadequate or missing. |

**Where band 6 becomes band 7:** linkers stop being bolted on. "Firstly...
Secondly... Moreover..." at the head of every sentence is the classic band-6
mechanical pattern — it *is* cohesion, but it attracts attention, which is
exactly what band 7 does not do.

## Lexical Resource

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | A wide resource used fluently and flexibly to convey precise meaning. Skilful use of uncommon or idiomatic items, despite occasional inaccuracy in word choice or collocation. Occasional spelling or word-formation slips with minimal impact. |
| **7** | Enough resource for some flexibility and precision. Some ability with less common or idiomatic items. Awareness of style and collocation is visible, though there are inappropriacies. Only a few spelling or word-formation errors, and they do not obscure meaning. |
| **6** | Generally adequate and appropriate for the task. Meaning is generally clear despite a restricted range or imprecise word choice. A risk-taker will show wider vocabulary but with more inaccuracy. Some spelling and word-formation errors, but they do not impede communication. |
| **5** | Limited but minimally adequate. Simple vocabulary may be accurate, but the range does not permit much variation. Frequent inappropriate word choice; noticeable spelling or word-formation errors that can cause the reader difficulty. |

**Where band 6 becomes band 7:** precision, not rarity. Band 7 is *the right
word*, not *the unusual word*. Thesaurus substitution that produces an
unnatural collocation moves you down, not up.

## Grammatical Range & Accuracy

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | A wide range of structures used flexibly and accurately. Most sentences error-free; punctuation well managed. Errors are occasional and non-systematic. |
| **7** | A variety of complex structures used with some flexibility and accuracy. Grammar and punctuation generally well controlled, and **error-free sentences are frequent**. A few errors persist but do not impede communication. |
| **6** | A mix of simple and complex forms, but limited flexibility. Complex structures are **not as accurate as** the simple ones. Errors in grammar and punctuation occur but rarely impede communication. |
| **5** | Limited and repetitive range. Complex sentences are attempted but tend to be faulty; accuracy is best on simple sentences. Errors may be frequent and cause the reader some difficulty. Punctuation may be faulty. |

**Where band 6 becomes band 7:** frequency of clean sentences. Count them. If
fewer than roughly half your sentences are error-free, you are at 6, however
ambitious the structures are.

# WRITING TASK 2

Task 2 is **worth double** Task 1 in the Writing band. Coherence & Cohesion,
Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range & Accuracy work as above; the first
criterion changes.

## Task Response

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | The prompt is addressed appropriately and sufficiently. A clear, **well-developed** position is presented in response to the question. Ideas relevant, well extended and supported; only occasional omissions. |
| **7** | **The main parts of the prompt are addressed appropriately.** A clear, developed position is presented. Main ideas are extended and supported — but there may be a tendency to over-generalise, or supporting material may lack focus and precision. |
| **6** | The main parts are addressed, **though some more fully than others**. A position relevant to the prompt is presented, but conclusions may be unclear, unjustified or repetitive. Main ideas are relevant but some are underdeveloped or unclear, and some support is weak or less relevant. |
| **5** | The main parts are **incompletely addressed**. A position is expressed but its development is not always clear. Main ideas are limited, insufficiently developed, or padded with irrelevant detail. Some repetition. |

**Where band 6 becomes band 7:** answer *every* part of the question, with equal
weight, and develop each main idea rather than asserting it. A two-part question
("discuss both views **and** give your opinion") where the opinion is a single
final sentence is a band-6 response.

**The band-7 killers, in order of how often they cost marks:**
1. One part of the question left unanswered or half-answered.
2. Main ideas asserted but never developed — no reason, no example, no
   consequence.
3. Memorised openers and templates ("In this modern era of globalisation...")
   which are transparent to examiners and count as neither Task Response nor
   Lexical Resource.
4. Linker spam — a discourse marker on every sentence.
5. Informal register: contractions, *you*, slang, rhetorical questions in bulk.
6. Under length (<250 words) — a direct penalty.

# SPEAKING

Four criteria, weighted equally.

> **Source note.** This section is written from the **current** Speaking
> descriptors (ielts.org, PDF created September 2025). The copy that dominates
> search results — hosted on a Cambridge English webinars path — is a **2013**
> edition and its wording differs materially, especially on Pronunciation. If you
> compare this against something you found online and it disagrees, check the
> edition date of what you found.

## Fluency & Coherence

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | Fluent, with only very occasional repetition or self-correction. Hesitation may occasionally be used to find words or grammar, but **most hesitation is content-related**. Topic development is coherent, appropriate and relevant. |
| **7** | Keeps going and readily produces long turns **without noticeable effort**. Some hesitation, repetition or self-correction occurs — often **mid-sentence, and it does signal difficulty accessing the right language** — but it does **not** damage coherence. Flexible use of spoken discourse markers, connectives and cohesive features. |
| **6** | Keeps going and shows willingness to produce long turns, but **coherence is sometimes lost** through hesitation, repetition or self-correction. Uses discourse markers and connectives, though not always appropriately. |
| **5** | Usually keeps going, but relies on repetition, self-correction or slow speech to do it. Hesitations often come mid-sentence while searching for **fairly basic** vocabulary and grammar. Overuses certain discourse markers. Simple speech can be fluent; complex speech usually breaks down. |

**Where band 6 becomes band 7:** not the absence of language-hunting — the
current descriptor explicitly says band 7 hesitation still signals difficulty
finding language. What changes is that **coherence survives it**. Band 6 loses
the thread; band 7 hesitates mid-sentence and still lands the point.

*(An earlier version of this file said band 7 hesitation is about ideas rather
than words. That is the band **8** boundary. Corrected against the 2025 text.)*

## Lexical Resource

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | Wide resource used readily and flexibly for precise meaning; skilful use of less common and idiomatic vocabulary, with occasional inaccuracy. Paraphrases effectively as needed. |
| **7** | Resource used flexibly across a variety of topics. Some less common and idiomatic vocabulary, with some awareness of style and collocation — and some inappropriate choices. **Paraphrases effectively.** |
| **6** | Vocabulary is wide enough to talk at length and make meaning clear, despite inappropriacies. **Generally paraphrases successfully.** |
| **5** | Manages familiar and unfamiliar topics but with limited flexibility. Attempts paraphrase with mixed success. |

**Where band 6 becomes band 7:** paraphrase moves from "generally successful" to
"effective", and collocation awareness appears. Not knowing a word is fine — the
band is decided by what you do next.

## Grammatical Range & Accuracy

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **8+** | Wide range used flexibly; a majority of error-free sentences; only very occasional inappropriacies or non-systematic errors. |
| **7** | A range of complex structures with some flexibility. **Frequently produces error-free sentences**, though some mistakes persist. |
| **6** | A mix of simple and complex structures with limited flexibility. May make **frequent mistakes with complex structures**, though these rarely block comprehension. |
| **5** | Basic forms are reasonably accurate. A limited range of complex structures, which usually contain errors and can cause comprehension problems. |

## Pronunciation

The 2025 descriptor rewrote this criterion substantially. It now talks about
**phonological features** and names specific ones — chunking, stress-timing,
rhythm, intonation — rather than "pronunciation features" in the abstract.

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **9** | A full range of phonological features conveying precise and subtle meaning. Connected speech sustained throughout. Effortless to understand. **Accent has no effect on intelligibility.** |
| **8** | A wide range of phonological features conveying precise meaning. Sustains appropriate **rhythm**; flexible stress and intonation across long utterances, with occasional lapses. Easily understood throughout. **Accent has minimal effect on intelligibility.** |
| **7** | All the positive features of band 6, plus some — but not all — of band 8. |
| **6** | A range of phonological features, but **control is variable**. Chunking is generally appropriate, but **rhythm may suffer from a lack of stress-timing or from speaking too fast**. Some effective intonation and stress, but not sustained. Individual words or phonemes may be mispronounced, causing occasional loss of clarity. Can generally be understood without much effort. |
| **5** | All of band 4 plus some of band 6. |

**The point that matters:** the criterion is **intelligibility**, not accent —
and the descriptor says so explicitly, mentioning accent only at bands 8 and 9
and only to say it has minimal or no effect there. A Tunisian or
French-influenced accent costs nothing by itself.

**What the 2025 wording tells you to actually work on**, in order:

1. **Speech rate.** Band 6 names *a rapid speech rate* as something that damages
   rhythm. Speaking fast to seem fluent is explicitly a band-6 marker. Slow down.
2. **Stress-timing.** English is stress-timed; Arabic and French are not. This is
   the single highest-value pronunciation feature for you, and it is now written
   into the descriptor by name.
3. **Chunking** — grouping words into sense units with pauses at the boundaries.
   Band 6 gets this "generally appropriate"; band 8 sustains it across long
   utterances.
4. **Intonation and stress that are *sustained*,** not just occasionally right.
   The band 6→8 difference is consistency, not capability.
5. Individual phonemes matter least of these four — they cost you only when they
   make a word unrecognisable.

Bands 5 and 7 are defined by reference to their neighbours, so pronunciation is
judged holistically: band 7 is "all of band 6 plus some of band 8". To move from
6 to 7 you need to start showing band-8 features, not merely stop making band-6
errors.

# How to combine bands

**Writing band** = (Task 1 band + 2 × Task 2 band) ÷ 3, rounded to the nearest
half band.

**Overall band** = mean of Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking, rounded to
the nearest half band. An average ending in .25 rounds up to the next half band;
.75 rounds up to the next whole band.

Worked example: 6.5 / 6.5 / 5.0 / 7.0 → average 6.25 → reported **6.5**.

**What band 7.0 overall actually requires.** You do not need 7 in everything.
7 / 7 / 6.5 / 6.5 averages 6.75 → rounds to **7.0**. So does 7.5 / 7 / 6 / 6.5.
The cheapest route for most candidates is Listening and Reading, where the marks
are objective: 30/40 in each is band 7 and is a training problem, not a talent
problem. Writing is the slowest to move — start it earliest.
