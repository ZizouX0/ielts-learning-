# Appendix C — Arabic/French error quick-reference card

**Print this. Keep it beside the keyboard for every timed essay.** It is a condensation of
Chapter 8 §L1 alert, which is the canonical home of all of it — nothing here contradicts
that chapter, and where you want the mechanism, the corpus evidence or more examples, go
there.

**The one-line version: fix articles and full stops before you fix anything clever.**

---

## 1 · Articles — your single most expensive error

Both your languages push the same way. French *la société* and Arabic *al-mujtamaʿ* both
put an article on a generic noun; English uses the zero article. This is the largest
measured band 6→7 discontinuity in the IELTS research literature — **18.4% error at band 6
falling to 7.4% at band 7**, with bands 5 and 6 not significantly different.
`[src: Roothooft & Breeze 2019, IELTS Research Reports 2019/1 — Speaking data, L1 mix unreported]`

**The rule is not "avoid *the*". It is "no *the* with a generic."**

> ✗ *The society expects…* → ✓ *Society expects…*
> ✗ *Governments must invest in the education to reduce the poverty.*
> → ✓ *Governments must invest in education to reduce poverty.*

**But *the* comes straight back** the moment the noun is made specific — by an *of*-phrase,
a defining relative clause, a superlative, an ordinal, a date, or a second mention:

> ✓ ***The** society **in which we live** rewards specialists.*
> ✓ ***The** number of students* · ***The** highest figure* · ***The** measures **that were introduced***

### ⚠ THE COUNTER-LIST — abstract nouns that KEEP their article

Do not strip these. There is no principle behind the split; learn it as a list.

| Take **the** in generic use | Take **zero** in generic use |
|---|---|
| the environment · the government · the media · the economy · the internet · the public · the police · the countryside · the weather · the climate · the authorities · the state · the past · the future · the elderly · the young | society · nature · technology · education · health · poverty · crime · unemployment · inflation · pollution · globalisation · tourism · agriculture · industry · science · history · art · music · life · knowledge · research · transport |

> ✗ *Environment is the main casualty…* → ✓ ***The** environment is the main casualty…*
> ✗ *Media exaggerate the risks…* → ✓ ***The** media exaggerate the risks…*

**Over-correction after this rule is taught is a real and predictable failure mode.** If you
find yourself deleting *the* from *the government*, stop and re-read this box.

### The reverse error — the missing *a/an*

Arabic has **no indefinite article**, so the slot simply stays empty.

> ✗ *Social media has great impact…* → ✓ *…has **a** great impact…*
> ✗ *He works as engineer.* → ✓ *…as **an** engineer.*
> ✗ *important for health of the body* → ✓ *…for **the** health of the body* (`the + N + of + N` is specific by definition)

**Five patterns cover most of what you actually get wrong:**
`the + NOUN + of + NOUN` is almost always *the* · superlatives and ordinals take *the* ·
generic plural is zero however long the modifier · percentages and dates take no article
(*in 2010*, *20 per cent of students* — but *the percentage of*, *the 1990s*) · second
mention takes *the*.

## 2 · The nine uncountables — the error that never resolves on its own

**information · advice · research · equipment · feedback · knowledge · software ·
furniture · evidence**

Never plural. Never *a/an*. French pluralises all of them; Arabic pluralises several.

This one gets a permanent checklist slot rather than a lesson. Overuse errors against the
plural (*peoples*, *informations*) were the **only** error type the IELTS study found at
**every** band level, explicitly reported as not resolving as proficiency rises. It survives
to band 8. It will not fix itself with practice.
`[src: Roothooft & Breeze 2019 — Speaking data, L1 mix unreported]`

| ✗ | ✓ |
|---|---|
| *several informations* | *a good deal of information* / *several **pieces of** information* |
| *many useful advices* | *a great deal of useful advice* / *a piece of advice* |
| *Recent researches show* | *Recent **research shows*** (the verb changes too) / *Recent **studies show*** |
| *modern equipments* | *modern equipment* |
| *the feedbacks they receive* | *the feedback they receive* |
| *technical knowledges* | *technical knowledge* (but ✓ *a working **knowledge of** statistics*) |
| *many evidences* | *considerable evidence* / *several **findings*** |
| *expensive softwares* | *expensive software* |
| *second-hand furnitures* | *second-hand furniture* |

And the one that is not on the list because it is not uncountable — ***peoples*** is a real
word meaning ethnic groups, and it is never what you mean. *People* is already plural:
✗ *Peoples are depend on…* → ✓ *People depend on…*

## 3 · Prepositions — a closed list, so learn it as one

| ✗ | ✓ | From |
|---|---|---|
| depend **of** | depend **on** | *dépendre de* |
| participate **to** | participate **in** | *participer à* |
| discuss **about** | discuss **Ø** (transitive) | *discuter de* |
| married **with** | married **to** | *marié avec* |
| arrive **to** the capital | arrive **in** a city/country, **at** a building/event | *arriver à* |
| interested **by** | interested **in** | *intéressé par* |
| responsible **of** / **about** | responsible **for** | *responsable de* / Arabic *ʿan* |
| consist **in** | consist **of** | *consister en* |
| suffer **of** | suffer **from** | *souffrir de* |
| contributed **on** | contributed **to** | Arabic transfer |
| focuses **in** | focuses **on** | Arabic transfer |
| **in** the other hand | **on** the other hand | fixed phrase |

**And *since* vs *for*** — French *depuis* and Arabic *mundhu* cover both.
*Since* + a **point** in time · *for* + a **length** of time.
✗ *running since ten years* → ✓ *for ten years* · ✗ *risen for 2015* → ✓ *since 2015*

## 4 · False friends — these cost **LR**, not GRA, so clean grammar cannot offset them

| You write | You mean | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| *actually* | currently | **at present**, currently |
| *the actual situation* | the present one | **the current** situation |
| *eventually* | possibly | **possibly**, potentially |
| *assist to* | to attend | **attend** |
| *demand somebody to* | to ask | **ask** somebody to |
| *formation* | training | **training** |
| *sensible* | sensitive | **sensitive** |
| *deception* | disappointment | **disappointment** |
| *library* (for a shop) | bookshop | **bookshop** |
| *support* | to tolerate | **tolerate**, put up with |
| *achieve* (a dissertation) | to finish | **complete**, finish |
| *control* | to check/inspect | **check**, inspect |
| ***significative*** | — **not an English word** | **significant** |
| *inconvenient* (as a noun) | drawback | **drawback**, disadvantage |
| *stage* | internship | **internship**, placement |
| ***permit to** study* | — **ungrammatical in every register** | **allow students to** study / **enable** X **to** / **make it possible to** |

That last row is grammar as well as vocabulary: English *permit* and *allow* need an
**object** before the infinitive. If you cannot supply one, change the verb.

**The false-friend five, for a 10-second scan:** *actually · eventually · assist · permit to
· significative.* Each is wrong more often than right in your writing.

## 5 · Full stops — comma splices and run-ons

**Punctuation is inside GRA**, so a splice is a grammar error that damages two clauses at
once. Arabic prose builds by coordination (*wa-*, *fa-*) rather than subordination; French
licenses a longer sentence. You get the length from one language and the chaining from the
other.

> ✗ *Public transport is cheap and it is used by many workers and it reduces traffic and it also reduces pollution and the government wants to expand it.*
>
> ✓ *Public transport in the capital is cheap and heavily used by workers. Because it takes cars off the road, it reduces both congestion and pollution — which is why the government intends to expand it.*

**The check:** read only the commas. At each one, cover the left — is the right a complete
sentence? Cover the right — is the left? Two complete sentences with a bare comma between
them is a splice.

**Repair with a subordinator if you can** (*because, although, while, since, whereas*) — that
converts an error into a complex structure. A full stop only if you cannot.

**And the adverb trap:** *however · therefore · moreover · thus · nevertheless* are adverbs.
They **cannot** join two clauses. The mark before them must be a full stop or a semicolon,
never a comma. *and · but · because · although · while* are conjunctions and can.

## 6 · Third-person *-s* — *occasional slip*, not *habitual omission*

The descriptors do permit a few basic errors to persist even at bands 7 and 8, so perfection
is not required. But the measured collapse is from **70.6% error at band 6 to 7.6% at band
7** — the sharpest cliff of any morpheme.
`[src: Roothooft & Breeze 2019 — Speaking data, L1 mix unreported]`

**The tolerance is real once your rate is occasional, and not before.** *A few* is a density
claim; the official criteria rate accuracy on error density.

> ✗ *Technology play an important role* → ✓ *Technology play**s***
> ✗ *A child who spend three hours online read less* → ✓ *A child who spend**s**… read**s** less* — **the verb inside the relative clause agrees too**, and that slot is the one usually missed
> ✗ *Each of the three regions have…* → ✓ *…**has**…* (*each* is singular; the plural in the *of*-phrase is a distractor)

**Do not overcorrect collectives.** *The government have decided…* is standard British
English. Leave it.

**And never stack two bare verbs:** ✗ *peoples are depend*, ✗ *he do not works*,
✗ *the government are decide*.

## 7 · Arabic-only: the relative-clause pair

Modern Standard Arabic leaves a pronoun in the relative gap, and drops the relative marker
after an indefinite head. So it predicts an **extra** pronoun *and* a **missing** one.

> ✗ *The reform which the ministry announced **it** in 2019…* → ✓ delete *it*
> ✗ *The district where the council built the tramline **in it**…* → ✓ delete *in it*
> ✗ *There are many households live below the poverty line.*
> → ✓ *…many households **that** live…* / *…many households **living** below the poverty line.*

**Learn the last one first.** The reduced-relative repair deletes a transfer error *and*
produces a band-7 participle clause in the same stroke.

**Copula omission** — high severity, low frequency: ✗ *These measures very expensive* →
✓ *These measures **are** very expensive.*

---

## 8 · Register — the French habit that is a CC cost, not a GRA one

*De plus · en outre · par ailleurs* transfer into *Moreover* or *Furthermore* at the head of
every paragraph. Band 6 names cohesion that is faulty or mechanical through misuse,
**overuse** or omission.

**Rule of thumb: if a linker could be deleted without the reader losing the thread, delete
it.** About two markers per paragraph, never zero.

And do not confuse elevation with precision:

> ✗ *In the name of progress, it is of the utmost significance to recognise that the technology has become a fundamental pillar of the modern educational sector.*
> ✓ *Technology has become central to modern education.*

Band 7 LR asks for awareness of style and collocation. It does not reward grandeur.

## The timed sweeps — rehearse these until they are automatic

They only work under the clock if you have done them fifty times.

**The 60-second Arabic sweep**
1. **Every `the` (25 s).** Is the noun after it an abstract idea in general, with no
   *of*-phrase, relative clause or superlative attached? Delete — **unless it is on the
   counter-list**.
2. **Every article-less singular noun (25 s).** Needs *a/an* or *the*. Proper nouns exempt.
3. **Every `which` / `who` / `where` (10 s).** Stray pronoun after the verb? Delete. Clause
   with no relative pronoun after an indefinite head? Insert *that*, or reduce to *-ing*.

**The 45-second French sweep**
1. **The nine words (15 s).** Scan for any carrying an *-s*. Delete it, fix the verb behind it.
2. **Verb + preposition (15 s).** depend **on** · participate **in** · discuss **Ø** ·
   married **to** · arrive **in/at** · responsible **for** · interested **in** · consist
   **of** · suffer **from**.
3. **The false-friend five (10 s).** *actually · eventually · assist · permit to · significative*.
4. **`since` / `for` (5 s).** Point in time → *since*. Length of time → *for*.

**The three-minute self-edit** — Task 1 stop at **17 minutes**, Task 2 stop at **37
minutes**. Do not read for meaning; you will read what you meant, not what you wrote.

> **Pass 1 — full stops (60 s).** §5 above.
> **Pass 2 — nouns (60 s).** Nine uncountables with an *-s*? Generic *the*? Bare singular
> countable? Anything in the frame `___ of ___` (it almost certainly needs *the*)?
> **Pass 3 — verbs (45 s).** Third-person *-s*, including inside relative clauses. Plural
> verb after *people/governments/students*. Two bare verbs stacked. A passive on an
> intransitive verb (*was happened*, *was risen*).
> **Pass 4 — your longest two or three sentences only (15 s).** *Although … but*? Delete the
> *but*. *Despite* + subject + verb? Participle clause — is the word right after the comma
> the thing doing the participle? *which … it*? *Would* in an *if*-clause?

**The rule that makes it work: in these three minutes you may delete and repair. You may not
add.** An untested complex sentence at minute 39 is the likeliest place for a faulty one to
appear — and one faulty complex sentence re-instates the band-6 limiter you spent the whole
essay avoiding.

## Your error map, ranked by expected cost

At the 6→7 boundary both bands sit above the impede-communication line, so **severity is not
what separates them — density is.**

| # | Error | Criterion |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Articles — generic *the*, missing *a/an*, missing *the* before *N of N* | GRA |
| 2 | Comma splices and run-ons | GRA (+CC) |
| 3 | Third-person *-s* and subject–verb agreement | GRA |
| 4 | Uncountables pluralised | GRA, arguably LR too |
| 5 | Faulty complex structures — *Although…but*, *despite* + clause, danglers, resumptives | GRA — **this is the band-6 limiter** |
| 6 | Prepositions | GRA |
| 7 | False friends and word-formation errors | **LR** |
| 8 | Tense consistency; *since* vs *for* | GRA |
| 9 | Copula omission | GRA |
| 10 | Linker overuse, over-formal register, repetition | CC / TR |

**Nothing on that list is a bolded rating-limiter in the descriptor grid** — bold starts at
band 5 — and nothing needs to be. Band 6 is defined by its positive features, and these are
what stop you fitting band 7's.
