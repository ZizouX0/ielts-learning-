# Cheat sheets — the final 48 hours

Five sheets, one per paper. Each fits a printed page.

This is not for learning. Everything here is taught properly in the chapters —
these are the pages you read when you are too anxious to read prose.

---

# 1 · LISTENING

**40 questions · 1 mark each · ~30 min · played ONCE · no negative marking**

| Band | Marks |
|---:|---|
| 6 | 23–25 |
| **6.5** | **26–29** |
| **7** | **30–31** |
| 7.5 | 32–34 |

**From the top of 6.5 to band 7 is ONE mark.**

### The four rules that pay

1. **One answer per gap.** Two scores zero even when one is right.
2. **Never a blank.** No negative marking. Guess the right *type* of word.
3. **Obey the word limit exactly.** Three words under a two-word limit = zero.
   Articles and prepositions count; hyphenated words are one.
4. **Answers come in order — every question type.** Miss one, go forward. Hunting
   backwards turns one lost mark into three.

### Predict before every gap
Type of word · singular or plural · two candidate answers.
*"The library closes at ______"* can only take a time.

### The traps
- Option mentioned **then rejected** — *we did consider Thursday, but…*
- Correction **after** — *sorry, I mean…* → the answer follows
- Contrast **before** — *just €6, down from the usual €9.50* → the answer leads
- *proposed* ≠ *actual* · *modern* ≠ *recently opened*
- The item **missing** from a printed list is the answer

### Spelling
American spellings **accepted**. Capitalisation doesn't matter. But
*informations*, *researches*, *advices* are **zeros** — in Listening your writing
errors are the whole mark, not a criterion.

🖥️ **No transfer time. 2 minutes to check** — spelling and plurals only.

> **Walking in:** type it right the first time. There is no tidying-up phase.

# 2 · READING

**3 passages · 40 questions · 60 min · NO transfer time · no negative marking**

| Band | Marks |
|---:|---|
| 6 | 23–26 |
| **6.5** | **27–29** |
| **7** | **30–32** |

**Reading is harder than Listening at the same raw score.** 26 marks is 6.5 in
Listening and still 6 in Reading.

### The clock is the skill
About **20 minutes per passage**. Hard stops. Obey them mid-question.
Overrunning Passage 1 by ten minutes to win two marks costs five in Passage 3.

### Order
Matching Headings **early** — it maps the passage.
Matching Information **last** — slowest, answers not in order.

### True/False/Not Given — the one that decides your band

**Ask: can I point at the sentence that contradicts this?**

- **Yes** → FALSE
- **No** → **NOT GIVEN**
- Stated in different words → TRUE

**A scope mismatch is usually NOT GIVEN.** Passage gives a figure for *managerial
staff*, statement says *all employees* → the passage never spoke about the wider
group. That is a **gap**, not a contradiction.
Only FALSE when the passage *does* address it and goes the other way — *only in
coastal regions* vs *nationwide*.

**Never use outside knowledge.** Never reflex-guess Not Given — decide from what
you read.

🖥️ **Copy-paste from the passage** into completion answers. Spelling errors
become impossible. **CTRL+F does not work.**

> **Walking in:** the answer is never in the question's words. Hunt the idea.

# 3 · WRITING TASK 1

**≥150 words · 20 min · one third of the Writing band**

### The clock
| | |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Read, spot the pattern, decide the grouping |
| 0:05–0:17 | Write |
| **0:17** | **Stop writing** |
| 0:17–0:20 | Check |

### Four paragraphs
1. **Intro** — one sentence, paraphrase the prompt
2. **Overview** — 2–3 sentences, **no numbers**, the big patterns. *Overall,…*
3. **Body 1** — one group, with figures
4. **Body 2** — the rest

**No conclusion. No opinions. No causes.**

### The two things that cap Task Achievement
- **No overview** → capped.
- **Listing instead of grouping** → band 7 requires the data *appropriately
  categorised*. Walking along the x-axis is band 6 **by the wording**.

### Before you stop
Overview present? · ≥150 words (after subtracting anything copied)? · figures and
units right? · grouped not listed? · tense right? · no opinion?

**Copied rubric is discounted before your words are counted.** Copying the chart
title is the commonest silent way under 150.

> **Walking in:** decide the grouping before you write a word. Everything follows
> from it.

# 4 · WRITING TASK 2

**≥250 words · 40 min · WORTH DOUBLE TASK 1**

### The clock
| | |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Underline **every part** of the question. Plan. |
| 0:05–0:37 | Write |
| **0:37** | **Stop writing** |
| 0:37–0:40 | Check — delete and repair, **never add** |

### Structure
Intro (position stated) → Body 1 → Body 2 → Conclusion (no new ideas)

**Target 260–290. There is a floor and no ceiling** — an official 7.5 script runs
to ~375 words. Write as long as you can write *accurately*.

### The one thing that separates 6 from 7
**Develop, don't assert.**
> claim → **because** → **so what** → concrete instance

Band 6 states ideas. Band 7 extends them.

### The killers, in order
1. A question part unanswered → **"If you don't discuss both, you will be limited
   to Band 5"** (official)
2. Undeveloped paragraphs — claims with no reasoning
3. Memorised openers — *In this contemporary era of globalisation…*
4. Linker spam — **two discourse markers per paragraph is the ceiling**
5. Thesaurus abuse — the wrong rare word scores **below** the right common one

### Hedge, don't overgeneralise
*tend to · in most cases · are likely to · appears to be*
This is credited as higher-level lexis **and** defuses the band-7 "tendency to
over-generalise". It costs nothing and uses words you already own.

**Under length has no published penalty** — it costs you through Task Response
and reduced evidence of range.

> **Walking in:** answer every part of the question. Nothing else costs as much.

# 5 · SPEAKING

**11–14 min · 3 parts · four equal criteria**

### Part 1 — 4–5 min
**Answer + reason + example.** Two or three sentences. Never one word.

### Part 2 — 1 min prep, speak 1–2 min
Prep minute = **keywords, not sentences.** Reading aloud flattens delivery.
**Speak the full two minutes.** Being stopped **means you spoke long enough**.
Invent freely — nobody verifies your story.

### Part 3 — 4–5 min — where band 7 is decided
Expect pushback. It is a test, not hostility. **Concede, then hold your ground
with a reason.**

*It's likely that… · That's true up to a point, but… · Compared with a
generation ago… · Broadly speaking…*

### Pronunciation — in this order
1. **Slow down.** A rapid speech rate is named at band 6 as damaging rhythm.
2. **Stress-timing.** English compresses unstressed syllables. Arabic and French
   don't. Your cheapest route out of 6.
3. **Chunking** — pause at sense boundaries, not mid-phrase.
4. Word stress — resist French final-syllable stress.

**Your accent costs nothing.** The descriptor mentions accent only at bands 8 and
9, and only to say it has minimal or no effect.

### Two things to stop worrying about
- **Hesitation.** Band 7 hesitation *is* language-related and mid-sentence. It
  only must not break your coherence. Content-only hesitation is band **8**.
- **Small grammar slips.** Bands 7 *and* 8 both allow a few basic errors to
  persist. Make complex sentences **land**; don't chase every `-s`.

Don't know a word? **Paraphrase.** That is band 7, not failure.

> **Walking in:** slow down, and answer the question you were actually asked.

# The arithmetic, once

**Overall = mean of four, rounded to the nearest half band.**
.25 rounds up to the next half band. .75 rounds up to the next whole band.

**You do not need 7 everywhere:**

| L | R | W | S | Reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.5 | 7.5 | 6.0 | 6.5 | **7.0** |
| 7.0 | 7.0 | 6.5 | 6.5 | **7.0** |
| 6.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 ✗ |

From a 6.5 floor in all four, you need **two more half-bands** — not four sevens.
