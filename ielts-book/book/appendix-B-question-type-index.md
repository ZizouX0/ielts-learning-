# Appendix B — Complete question-type index

**What this is for.** You are in a practice paper and something unfamiliar has appeared.
Find it in the left-hand column, go to the section named on the right, read that section
and nothing else. Every entry points at the place where the type is taught with its own
trap list and its own band-6 mistake.

**A word on the counts before you use them.** The official type counts and this book's
counts differ in two places, and neither is a disagreement:

- **Listening:** ielts.org's format page names **six** types. This book works through
  **eleven layouts**, because the completion family splits into five printed layouts that
  behave differently on screen, and multiple choice splits into its one-answer and
  multiple-answer forms — a *choose TWO* item occupies two question numbers and is worth
  two marks. 5 + 2 + 4 = 11. `[src: ielts.org — IELTS Academic: Listening test format]`
- **Reading:** ielts.org's format page defines **11** numbered task types; the 2023
  sample-tasks PDF names **14** by splitting the completion formats apart. Both are
  official. **This book uses the 11**, because Cambridge files its own teaching material
  under that numbering. `[src: ielts.org — Reading test format; ielts.org — Academic Reading Sample Tasks 2023 PDF, p.1]`

One thing you cannot budget for: ielts.org answers *"How many questions are there?"* with
**"Variable"** for all eleven Reading types `[src: ielts.org — Reading test format]`.

## B.3 — Writing Task 1: the nine visual types

20 minutes · at least 150 words · a third of the Writing band. The instruction never
changes: **summarise, select, compare**.

| # | Visual type | Taught in |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Line graph | **Ch 4 §1. Line graph** |
| 2 | Bar chart | **Ch 4 §2. Bar chart** |
| 3 | Pie chart | **Ch 4 §3. Pie chart** |
| 4 | Table | **Ch 4 §4. Table** |
| 5 | Process diagram — man-made | **Ch 4 §5. Process diagram — man-made** |
| 6 | Process diagram — natural (cycle / life cycle) | **Ch 4 §6. Process diagram — natural** |
| 7 | Map comparison | **Ch 4 §7. Map comparison** |
| 8 | Mixed / multiple charts | **Ch 4 §8. Mixed / multiple charts** |
| 9 | Static diagram — an object, a device, or an event | **Ch 4 §9. Static diagram** |

**The nine-way split is a teaching convention, not an official taxonomy.** The official list
of inputs is *"a diagram, graph, table, chart, map or other visual input"*, and the format
page adds *"a diagram of an object, device, process or event"* — which is why **object** and
**device** are separate from **process**, and why there are nine sections and not eight.
`[src: ielts.org — Writing key assessment criteria PDF, p.2; ielts.org — IELTS Academic format: Writing]`

**"Or other visual input" is an open category by construction.** If you meet something that
fits none of the nine, do not reach for a template. Ask two questions, in order:

1. **Is there a time axis?** Yes → trend task: trend verbs legal, tense set by the dates,
   group by trend behaviour. No → trend verbs are **banned**; your organising principle is
   comparison (§2, §4, §9).
2. **Is there a sequence?** Yes → process: stages, sequencers, present simple passive, group
   into phases (§5, §6). Neither → §9.

## B.4 — Writing Task 2: the five question families

40 minutes · at least 250 words · two thirds of the Writing band · about **17% of your
entire IELTS result**.

> ⚠️ **These families are a recognition heuristic, not an official taxonomy.** IELTS
> publishes none — not on the format pages, not in the sample tasks PDF, not in the key
> assessment criteria, not in the descriptors. The two official owners' teaching pages both
> publish lists and they do not match: the British Council teaches five categories, IDP six
> `[src: takeielts.britishcouncil.org — How to write an English essay for IELTS; ielts.idp.com — How to understand Task 2 writing questions]`.
> The families are reliable shapes and they save planning time. The rule that earns the
> marks is the official one: **count the instructions in the prompt and answer every one.**
> Memorise five templates, meet a hybrid prompt, and you will misfire.

| # | Family | Recognise it by the final line | Demands | Taught in |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Opinion (agree / disagree) | *To what extent do you agree or disagree?* | 1 | **Ch 5 §Family 1 — Opinion** |
| 2 | Discussion (both views + your own opinion) | *Discuss both views and give your own opinion.* | 3 | **Ch 5 §Family 2 — Discussion** |
| 3 | Problem, cause and solution | *What are the causes? What can be done?* | 2 | **Ch 5 §Family 3 — Problem, cause and solution** |
| 4 | Advantages and disadvantages | *Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?* | 2 | **Ch 5 §Family 4 — Advantages and disadvantages** |
| 5 | Two-part / direct question | Two questions of different shapes | 2 | **Ch 5 §Family 5 — Two-part / direct question** |

**The 30-second triage:** the family is decided by the *final instruction line* — not the
topic, not the statement. Find the last sentence containing a question mark or an
imperative and count the separate demands. That count is the number of things your essay
must visibly deliver. Two families at once means a hybrid: **obey the demand count, not the
family name.** Full triage table: **Ch 5 §Question types, one by one**.
