# SPEAKING — the moves

**The shape:** Part 1 (4–5 min, 2–3 sentences per answer) · Part 2 (1 min prep,
speak 2 min) · Part 3 (4–5 min, opinions and pushback).

**1. Talk in Part 2 until the examiner stops you.**
Aim past two minutes. Being cut off is a receipt, not a rebuke.
→ *Stopping at 50 seconds forfeits the evidence band 7 needs.*

**2. Answer, reason, example — every Part 1 question.**
Two to three sentences. Never a bare yes or no. Extend before you are asked why.
→ *Flat two-clause answers are the band-6 signature.*

**3. Concede, then counter, whenever Part 3 pushes back.**
"Granted, X — even so, Y." Pushback is designed in. Disagreeing costs you nothing.
→ *Buys a concession clause and real depth in one breath.*

**4. Route round a missing word; never go silent.**
Define it, give its function, or give an example. Then keep moving.
→ *Paraphrase is scored. Not knowing the word is not.*

**5. Slow down. Speak measured, breathe between sentences.**
Fast wrecks your rhythm. Very slow wrecks fluency. Sit between them.
→ *Speed buys nothing and caps your pronunciation band.*

**6. Finish the sentence you started, even after a stall.**
Pause, breathe, complete the structure. Restart only if the idea is genuinely gone.
→ *Hesitation is allowed at band 7. Broken threads are not.*

**7. In the one minute, plan the tense and a spare.**
Keywords only, two or three per bullet, plus the tense and one backup idea.
→ *The spare line prevents the 50-second collapse.*

**8. Build every Part 3 answer in four steps.**
Answer → because → for instance → although / which means. The fourth step separates bands.
→ *Opinion, one reason, then stall — that is band 6.*

**9. Keep Part 3 general; use yourself only as an example.**
Talk about people, not your family. The examiner will steer you off personal ground.
→ *Personal answers in Part 3 cost you coherence marks.*

**10. Answer the rounding-off question in one sentence.**
Straight after Part 2, short is correct: answer plus one clause, then stop.
→ *A bare "yes" undoes two minutes of good English.*

**11. Ask for a repeat when you don't understand.**
Allowed in Parts 1 and 3. Ask twice at most, then answer anyway.
→ *Listening is not scored. Answering the wrong question is.*

**12. Mark your attitude in every Part 3 answer.**
"Worryingly", "to be fair", "it's encouraging that", "I'm not entirely convinced".
→ *Cheaper than rare vocabulary and worth more.*

**13. Use spoken links, never written ones.**
"The thing is", "mind you", "which is why", "that said". Drop "moreover" and "furthermore".
→ *Written connectives sound memorised the moment you say them.*

**14. Record every Part 2 and check three things.**
Speech rate, even beats, where your pauses land. Nothing else. Five minutes.
→ *The only feedback a book cannot give you.*

## Phrases to have ready

**Buying time**
- "Let me see…"
- "That's not something I've thought about much, actually."
- "Well, it depends, really."
- "How can I put this…"

**Giving an opinion**
- "I'd say…"
- "To be honest, I think…"
- "If you ask me…"
- "I'm not entirely convinced that…"

**Conceding then countering**
- "Granted, … even so, …"
- "True up to a point, though …"
- "I can see why people say that, although …"
- "There's something in that, but …"

**Comparing**
- "It's far more common than it was."
- "Compared with, say, ten years ago…"
- "Nothing like it used to be."
- "It's less about X than about Y."

**Speculating**
- "I'd imagine…"
- "That could well lead to…"
- "Chances are…"
- "Presumably…"

**Not knowing a word**
- "I don't know the technical term, but it's the thing you use to…"
- "It's a sort of…"
- "What's the word — something like…"
- "You know, the kind of…"

## Pronunciation — the only 3 things that matter for you

**Slow down.** Speak at the pace you'd use explaining something to a friend, and
breathe at the end of every sentence. Rushing is what flattens your rhythm.

**Stress-timing.** English lands its beats evenly and crushes everything between
them. Tap three beats on the table and force the words to fit: *CATS · EAT · FISH* →
*The CATS will EAT the FISH* → *The CATS would have EATen the FISH* — same time, every
line. "Would have" becomes "wud-uv"; "the" becomes "thuh".

**Chunking.** Pause only at the joins between meaning units, never inside one.
Read aloud stopping only at the slashes: *To be honest, / the thing I value most / is
an old watch / my grandfather gave me / when I turned eighteen.*

**The paper-strip drill for /p/.** Hold a thin strip of paper near your lips. Say
**pin – bin · pack – back · pear – bear · port – bought**. The paper must jump on every
*p* and stay still on every *b*. Two minutes a day. Your /p/ isn't missing — it's
missing air.

# LANGUAGE — the moves

**1. Remove errors before you add words.**
Cutting ten mistakes moves your band. Adding ten new words does not.
→ *You are marked on error density, not vocabulary size.*

**2. Hedge every sweeping claim.**
"Tend to", "appears to", "in most cases", "may well". Delete every *all / always / never*.
→ *The cheapest band-7 feature. Uses words you already own.*

**3. Learn phrases, never single words.**
"Place a strain on", "pose a threat to", "address a problem". Card front = phrase with a gap.
→ *Word partnerships are the assessed unit, not headwords.*

**4. Use compound nouns instead of adjectives you half-know.**
Traffic congestion · income inequality · screen time · public transport · life expectancy.
→ *Reads as topic command, near-zero error risk.*

**5. Choose the exact word, not the impressive one.**
"Use" beats "utilise". Number = countable, amount = uncountable, rate = per something.
→ *Reaching for a big word is the band-6 profile.*

**6. Never paraphrase a word that has no synonym.**
"People", "in conclusion", "carbon dioxide". Repeat them without guilt.
→ *A wrong substitution costs more than a repetition.*

**7. Learn your thirty spellings by rote.**
Government · development · environment · maintenance · separate · definitely · necessary · successful.
→ *Spelling is scored, and there is no spellchecker.*

**8. Repair every comma splice with a subordinator.**
Swap the comma for *because*, *although*, *while*, *since*.
→ *Turns a punctuation error into a complex structure.*

**9. Treat "however" as an adverb, not a joiner.**
Full stop before it, comma after. Same for *therefore*, *moreover*, *nevertheless*.
→ *Each one sitting in a splice kills two clauses.*

**10. Own the second conditional. Skip the third.**
*If governments taxed sugar, consumption would fall.* No *would* inside the if-clause.
→ *One pattern, drilled cold, beats three half-known ones.*

**11. Load your noun phrases before you add clauses.**
One modifier in front, one behind: *enrolment* → *the sharp fall in rural enrolment after 2015*.
→ *Free grammatical range with almost no failure mode.*

**12. Stop writing three minutes early and only subtract.**
Delete and repair. Never add a new sentence at the end.
→ *The clever last sentence is where band 7 dies.*

## Articles — the one that decides band 6 vs 7

**DROP "the"** (talking in general):
society · nature · technology · education · health · poverty · crime · unemployment ·
inflation · pollution · globalisation · tourism · agriculture · industry · science ·
history · art · music · life · knowledge · research · transport

**KEEP "the"** (even in general):
the government · the environment · the media · the economy · the internet · the public ·
the police · the countryside · the weather · the climate · the state · the authorities ·
the past · the future

**The check:** if you can add "in general" after the noun, delete the *the* — unless it
is on the KEEP list. The *the* comes straight back the moment an *of*-phrase, a relative
clause, a superlative or a second mention arrives: *the impact **of** technology*, *the
society **we live in***, *the highest figure*.

## The nine uncountables

**information · advice · research · equipment · feedback · knowledge · software ·
furniture · evidence**

Never plural. Never *a*. Never *many* or *fewer* — use *much*, *less*, *a great deal of*,
*a piece of*. The verb behind them is singular: *Recent research **shows***, not *researches
show*. Add *people* to the scan: never *peoples*.

## Your 60-second proofread

Run this in the last three minutes. Do not read for meaning — you will read what you
meant, not what you wrote.

1. **Commas.** Two complete sentences joined by a comma? Replace with *because /
   although / while*, or a full stop. Every *however / therefore / moreover* needs a full
   stop before it.
2. **Every "the".** Abstract noun, talked about in general, nothing attached after it?
   Delete — unless it is on the KEEP list.
3. **Every bare singular noun.** No article, no possessive, no quantifier? It needs *a/an*
   or *the*. And anything in the frame `___ of ___` almost always takes *the*.
4. **The nine uncountables.** Any of them carrying an *-s*? Delete it, then fix the verb
   behind it. Check *peoples* too.
5. **Verbs.** Singular subject in present simple — is the *-s* there, including inside
   relative clauses? Any two bare verbs stacked (*are depend*, *do not works*)?
6. **Your two longest sentences only.** *Although…but*? Delete the *but*. *Despite* +
   clause? Rewrite. *which…it* / *who…them*? Delete the pronoun. *Would* in an if-clause?
   Change to past simple.

**The rule that makes it work: in these three minutes you may delete and repair. You may
not add.**
