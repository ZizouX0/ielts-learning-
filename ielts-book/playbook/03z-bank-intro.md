# THE WRITING BANKS

**Do not read these. Look things up in them.**

The moves you just read are what you *do*. What follows is the language you use
to do it — organised by the job, not by the chart type.

**How to use them**

| When | Go to |
|---|---|
| You need to compare two figures | Task 1 bank → §10 Comparison |
| A number went up and you've already written "increased" twice | Task 1 bank → §3 and §8 |
| You don't know what tense to use | Task 1 bank → §14 |
| You got a process or a map | Task 1 bank → §15 or §16 |
| You're staring at a Task 2 question and have no ideas | Task 2 bank → §17 Idea banks |
| You need to disagree without sounding rude | Task 2 bank → §10 Concede-then-counter |
| Your sentence sounds too absolute | Task 2 bank → §11 Hedging |
| You need a structure and can't remember the pattern | Grammar on demand |

**Before your test**, read only two things here: the **comparison** section, because
the official Task 1 instruction demands comparisons, and the **concede-then-counter**
frames, because that single move is what makes a Task 2 argument look mature.

Everything else is for when you are stuck mid-drill.
