# LISTENING — the moves

**The arithmetic:** Band 6 = 23/40 · band 6.5 = 26–29 · **band 7 = 30**. From 29 the gap is one mark; from 23 it is seven. All of them are boxes holding the wrong string.

**1. Never write two answers in one box.**
Pick one. Hedging with both scores zero even when one is right.
→ *Turns a 50% chance into a certain zero.*

**2. Fill all forty boxes. Never leave one empty.**
No negative marking. Guess one answer of the right type and move on.
→ *Every blank is a zero you chose.*

**3. Miss a gap? Move on inside two seconds.**
Answers come in order for every type, matching and maps included. Type a guess, jump forward.
→ *Hunting backwards turns one lost mark into three.*

**4. Read the word limit above every single task.**
Limits change inside one paper. Under TWO WORDS, stop at two even if the third is true.
→ *One word over = zero on a correct answer.*

**5. Label every gap in the reading window.**
Write the category and word class: surname, price, number+unit, noun, verb.
→ *Converts listening into slot-matching. Worth 2–3 marks.*

**6. Learn the correction trap in both directions.**
*Sorry, I mean* = answer comes after. *Just six euros, usually nine fifty* = answer comes first.
→ *1–2 marks. Most candidates only know the first one.*

**7. Read all ten Part 4 questions in the opening window.**
There is no second window. The mid-part pause is unannounced and is not reading time.
→ *Protects up to 10 marks in one part.*

**8. Write the words you hear. Never paraphrase.**
There is no vocabulary credit in Listening. Your better synonym is a wrong answer.
→ *Every rephrase is a whole mark gone.*

**9. Type answers as you listen. Never take notes.**
The audio does not wait. Prose in the margin is a mark you did not type.
→ *Note-taking costs answers in Parts 3 and 4.*

**10. Never type an article you were not forced to type.**
*the*, *a*, *in*, *on* all count toward the word limit.
→ *Can cost a mark; can never gain one.*

**11. Target 10/10 on Part 1.**
Names, numbers, prices, spelled-out words. Slow, clear, twice-stated.
→ *Cheapest ten marks in the test. Never concede them.*

**12. Never add -s to English uncountables.**
*informations, researches, advices, equipments, feedbacks, softwares* — each is a straight zero.
→ *Your highest-frequency Listening spelling loss.*

**13. On maps, put your cursor on the entrance.**
Track the speaker's body, not the page. When you hear "turn", rotate.
→ *Fixing left/right to the page inverts half the plan.*

**14. Drill the spatial prepositions as a closed list.**
opposite · next to · adjacent to · beyond · past · behind · in front of · at the far end.
→ *In a map task the preposition is the answer.*

**15. Circle TWO or THREE before you read the options.**
A "choose TWO" item occupies two question numbers. Two numbers, two letters.
→ *Answering one letter throws away a mark.*

**16. Flag four questions maximum, never ten.**
On computer there is no transfer time — two minutes to check, three seconds per answer.
→ *Flag everything and you have flagged nothing.*

**17. Type normally. No ALL CAPS, no spelling anxiety.**
Capitalisation is ignored and American spellings are accepted — *meter*, *center*, *program*, *labor*.
→ *Saves keystrokes, kills typos, frees weeks of study time.*

**18. Train five accents, not two.**
UK, US, Australia, New Zealand, Canada. Sixty seconds of audio, write only numbers and names.
→ *Parts 2 and 4 are one voice each — ten marks apiece.*

## The 3 traps, in one line each

- **The loudest option is the rejection.** The name you hear clearest is usually the one being turned down — listen for the comparative (*the top one*, *the cheaper one*), not the names.
- **The late reversal.** Two sentences of enthusiasm, then four words that cancel it (*Forget that one, then*) — never commit before the final clause of an item.
- **Status words.** *Proposed* is not *actual* and *modern* is not *recently opened* — read what tense and status each gap demands before the audio starts.

# READING — the moves

**The arithmetic:** Band 6 = 23–26/40 · band 6.5 = 27–29 · **band 7 = 30**. From 27 the gap is three marks: one added article, one added plural, one answer over the limit.

**1. Copy-paste every completion answer.**
Select the word in the passage, copy, paste, then count words against the rubric.
→ *Makes spelling errors structurally impossible. Your biggest single block.*

**2. Never leave a blank. Reserve the last three minutes.**
Sweep every unanswered box and put something in it.
→ *Three marks if you currently time out on Passage 3.*

**3. Stop at twenty minutes per passage. Hard stop.**
Nineteen, nineteen, nineteen, plus a three-minute sweep. Ceiling of 90 seconds per question.
→ *Prevents the Passage 3 collapse that costs 5+ marks.*

**4. FALSE needs a sentence you can point at.**
No contradicting sentence, no FALSE. If you are building an argument, it is NOT GIVEN.
→ *The single biggest avoidable loss on the paper.*

**5. Write the word limit at the top of every set.**
It changes between sets in the same paper. Over the limit is marked wrong.
→ *Mechanical marks recovered with zero reading improvement.*

**6. Never read the passage through first.**
Survey the layout 20 seconds, skim first and last sentences, then scan only where the question sends you.
→ *Reading 2,500 words at comprehension speed eats your hour.*

**7. Vague in the text, precise in the statement: NOT GIVEN.**
Text says *numerous*, statement says *most* — no basis to confirm or deny.
→ *The item candidates fail most often.*

**8. Narrow in the passage, wide in the statement: NOT GIVEN.**
Unless the text says something about the extra part that rules the statement out — then FALSE.
→ *Costs several marks at once, because these cluster.*

**9. Learn which types run in passage order.**
Ordered: multiple choice, T/F/NG, Y/N/NG, sentence endings, sentence completion, short answer. Resume from the last answer.
→ *One scan instead of six.*

**10. Do matching information last, paragraph by paragraph.**
Read paragraph A, then look through all the points. Never question-by-question.
→ *Eight reads instead of forty-eight scans.*

**11. Matching headings: work the paragraphs top to bottom.**
The questions run in paragraph order; the Roman numerals are scrambled.
→ *Never expect heading (i) to belong to paragraph A.*

**12. Answer from the text before you uncover the options.**
Cover A–D, locate the region, say the answer in your own words, then match.
→ *Reading options first guarantees you find the distractor.*

**13. Never write one letter on a "Choose TWO" item.**
It is worth two marks and either order is accepted. Write your certain letter plus your best guess.
→ *A free mark thrown away every time.*

**14. Never write two answers in one gap.**
One correct answer inside the pair still scores zero.
→ *Zero on a question you had right.*

**15. Add no article, plural or preposition the gap did not ask for.**
The passage says *agate* — write *agate*, not *the agate*, not *polished agate*.
→ *Your two commonest Writing errors become whole marks here.*

**16. On word-bank summaries, stop searching the passage.**
The bank words are paraphrases and often appear nowhere in the text. Decide by meaning.
→ *Every option fits grammatically, so grammar decides nothing.*

**17. Read the subordinator before the clause.**
*Although the yield rose* and *Because the yield rose* give opposite T/F/NG answers.
→ *One word flips the answer on factual items.*

**18. Learn phrasal verbs before any subject glossary.**
*rule out · account for · bear out · bring about · carry out · put down to.*
→ *Exactly what question stems paraphrase with. Your best vocabulary hour.*

## The 3 traps, in one line each

- **Same words, wrong paragraph.** Distractors are built from the passage's own vocabulary — look for the information, not the words.
- **The absolute that is not FALSE.** *All / never / only* in a statement is not automatic FALSE; check whether a sentence actually excludes the case, otherwise it is NOT GIVEN.
- **French false friends flipping the answer.** *actually* = in fact · *eventually* = in the end · *sensible* = level-headed · *assist* = help · *support* = back · *formation* = shape.
