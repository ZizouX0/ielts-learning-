# Reading drill

## Step 1 — Target my weakness

Read `progress/error-log.md` and `progress/band-tracker.md`. Find which Reading
question type I get wrong most often. If there is no data, start with
**True/False/Not Given** — it is the type most candidates lose most marks on.

Say in one line which type you are targeting and why.

## Step 2 — Write an ORIGINAL passage

Write a **completely original** academic passage of **400–600 words**.

**Never copy or adapt a real IELTS passage, Cambridge test, or any published
article.** Write it from scratch.

Make it read like the real thing:
- Academic register, third person, no contractions
- A topic of the kind IELTS uses: history of science, archaeology, biology,
  urban planning, linguistics, economics, environmental studies, psychology
- 5–7 paragraphs, each with a clear main idea (so heading-matching is possible)
- Embedded specifics — dates, names, figures, technical terms — so keyword
  anchoring is trainable
- **Deliberate paraphrase distance:** the ideas that answer questions must be
  expressed in different words from the questions. That gap is the actual skill.
- At least two places where a statement is *plausible but simply not addressed*,
  so Not Given is genuinely available

Label paragraphs **A, B, C...** so paragraph-matching questions work.

## Step 3 — Write 8–10 questions

Weight them toward my weakest type — roughly 60% that type, 40% mixed for
realism.

Use authentic instruction wording and state word limits exactly as the real test
does (e.g. *NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER FROM THE PASSAGE*).

Give a time limit: **about 18 minutes** for a single passage with 10 questions.

Then **stop and wait** for my answers. Do not reveal anything.

## Step 4 — Mark strictly

Mark each answer right or wrong. No partial credit. Spelling errors and word-limit
breaches are **wrong**, exactly as on test day — say which rule was broken.

Report: **x/10**, and what that pace and accuracy would mean across 40 questions
(see the conversion table in `knowledge/tricks.md`).

## Step 5 — Show where every answer hid — the whole point of this drill

For **every** question, right or wrong:

> **Q4 — TRUE**
> **The sentence that held it:** "Excavations at the site yielded no ceramic
> fragments postdating the eighth century, a pattern repeated at three
> neighbouring settlements."
> **The paraphrase that disguised it:** the question said *pottery* where the
> text said *ceramic fragments*; the question said *abandoned by 800 CE* where
> the text said *nothing postdating the eighth century*. Two substitutions in one
> question.
> **Why not NOT GIVEN:** the text does make the claim — it just makes it
> negatively, through absence of evidence, which is stated, not inferred.

For wrong answers, name the specific trap I fell for:
- Chose False where the text was silent (→ Not Given)
- Used outside knowledge instead of the text
- Missed a qualifier (*all* / *some* / *most* / *only*)
- Matched a heading to a detail rather than the paragraph's main idea
- Picked an option that was true but did not answer the stem
- Broke the word limit

## Step 6 — Give me the transferable rule

Two or three sentences: what to do differently next time, stated as a rule I can
apply to a passage I have never seen.

## Step 7 — Update progress files and push

Log the score and the error pattern in `progress/band-tracker.md`, wrong-answer
patterns in `progress/error-log.md`, and any useful academic vocabulary from the
passage in `progress/vocab-bank.md`.

Commit and push:
`progress: reading — <question type> — x/10`

## Step 8 — End the session

One line: the single highest-impact fix.
