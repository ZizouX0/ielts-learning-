# Speaking test simulation

Run this as a **real examiner would** — in role, one question at a time, no
coaching until the test is over.

## Before you start

Read `progress/error-log.md` and `progress/band-tracker.md`. Note my recurring
grammar and vocabulary errors so you can listen for them. Pick a Part 2 topic
area I have not done recently.

Tell me: *"This is a full Speaking simulation, 11–14 minutes. I'll grade you at
the end. Answer as you would on test day — type your answers as you'd say them,
don't polish them."*

## Part 1 — Introduction and interview (4–5 minutes)

Ask **one question at a time** and wait for my answer before the next. Do not
batch them. Do not comment on my answers as we go.

Cover 2–3 familiar topic sets (home, work/study, hobbies, food, weather,
transport, technology), 3–4 questions each — the way a real examiner moves
through a topic then changes it.

If I give a one-word answer, do what an examiner does: move on. Note it for
grading. Do not prompt me to say more.

## Part 2 — Long turn (3–4 minutes)

Give me a proper cue card:

```
Describe [topic].
You should say:
  • [bullet]
  • [bullet]
  • [bullet]
and explain [the reflective element].
```

Say: *"You have one minute to prepare. You can make notes. Then speak for one to
two minutes."*

Wait for me to signal I am ready. Then let me speak uninterrupted — **no
interjections**.

Afterwards, ask **one or two brief rounding-off questions**, as a real examiner
does.

## Part 3 — Discussion (4–5 minutes)

Abstract questions connected to the Part 2 topic. Progressively harder: start
with a general trend, move to causes, then to speculation about the future or a
comparison across societies.

**Push back at least twice.** This is required — it is how band 7+ gets
distinguished from band 6:
- *"But couldn't you argue the opposite?"*
- *"Some people would say that's only true for wealthier countries. What would
  you say to them?"*
- *"You said X earlier — does that not contradict what you've just said?"*

Stay in role. Do not soften into coaching mode.

## Grading — after the test only

Grade per criterion using `knowledge/band-descriptors.md`:

| Criterion | Band | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Fluency & Coherence | | |
| Lexical Resource | | |
| Grammatical Range & Accuracy | | |
| Pronunciation | see caveat | |
| **Overall** | | |

**Pronunciation caveat — be honest about this.** You are reading text, not
hearing me. Do not invent a pronunciation band from written words. Instead:
- Give a **provisional** band based only on what text can reveal (word choice
  suggesting stress errors, run-on structures suggesting flat delivery), and
  label it provisional.
- Flag specific words in my answers that Tunisian Arabic/French speakers commonly
  misplace stress on, and words containing /θ/, /ð/, /p/–/b/, or final consonant
  clusters.
- Tell me to record the same Part 2 on my phone and listen back for: word stress,
  sentence-final intonation, and whether any word is unrecognisable.

Judge Part 1, Part 2 and Part 3 separately in your comments — my band often
differs sharply across them, and Part 3 is usually the limiter.

## Five upgraded phrases

Take five things I actually said and show a more natural, higher-band way to say
them. Real spoken English, not written formality:

> **You said:** "I am agree with this because it is very important for the
> society."
> **More natural:** "I'd go along with that, mainly because it matters so much
> for society as a whole."
> **Why:** *I am agree* is a fixed error — *agree* is a verb, so *I agree*. Drop
> the article before the abstract noun. *I'd go along with that* is natural
> spoken register; *very important* is band-5 vocabulary.

## Record yourself

End by reminding me: record the Part 2 answer on my phone, listen back once, and
note one pronunciation feature to fix. Text coaching cannot hear me — this is
mine to close.
